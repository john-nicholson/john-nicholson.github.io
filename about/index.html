About

I am a technology leader who has spent most of my career working where technology, organisations and change meet.

That has taken me through software engineering, cloud, architecture, DevOps, platform engineering, large-scale transformation and, increasingly, AI. Much of my work has been in complex or regulated environments where the interesting challenge is rarely choosing the technology. It is working out how to change the surrounding system without losing control of what matters.

That is also what I tend to write about here.

I am interested in flow: how work moves, where it gets stuck, what creates unnecessary friction and how organisations can make meaningful change more often. I am equally interested in architecture, but less as a collection of diagrams and more as a way of shaping capability, constraints and future choices.

More recently, a lot of my thinking has moved towards AI and agency. Not just models or individual use cases, but the wider questions: what context an AI system needs, how organisations encode knowledge and policy, how decisions should be made, what should remain deterministic, and where human judgement is genuinely necessary.

Those topics connect more than they first appear to.

A value stream, a cloud platform, an approval process, a knowledge graph and an agentic system are all, in different ways, attempts to shape how information and decisions move through a system. That is probably the common thread behind most of the writing on this site.

A few other things that shape the perspective
#

I have worked across consultancy, software businesses, education and public-sector-adjacent environments, and I have spent time on both sides of the supplier/client boundary. I am also involved in charity governance and church leadership. Those contexts are very different, but they repeatedly raise the same questions about trust, accountability, stewardship, risk and how much control is actually useful.

I also write occasionally about personal systems, learning and ADHD. Attention is a constraint too, and some of the same ideas that apply to organisational flow turn out to apply surprisingly well to individual work.

This is not intended to be a polished personal-brand site or a substitute for a CV. It is a place to develop and publish ideas, including some that are still changing.

The older posts are intentionally still here. They are part of the record of how my thinking has developed, even where I would write them differently now.

If you are looking for the conventional career summary, LinkedIn is the better place for that. This site is more about the ideas behind the work.

Any opinions expressed here are my own and do not represent any organisation I work for, have worked for, or advise.
