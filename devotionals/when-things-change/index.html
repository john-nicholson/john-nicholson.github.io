When Things Change

14 September 2026·1430 words·7 mins

Read: Scripture Psalm 46:1–7 NIV

Tap or hover over the reference to read the full passage without leaving the page.

Change can be difficult. Sometimes it is not even the change itself that is hardest, but the uncertainty that comes with it. We might know how yesterday worked, but suddenly we are not quite sure what tomorrow will look like.

That can happen when we start somewhere new, perhaps a new school, class or group. We need to learn where we fit, who people are and how things work. But change can happen in familiar places too. Someone we know might begin behaving differently towards us. A friendship might feel different. Something that once seemed easy can suddenly become confusing, and we can find ourselves wondering what we are supposed to do next.

The Bible never promises us a life where nothing changes. In fact, many of the people we read about in Scripture found themselves facing enormous uncertainty. Abraham left the place he knew without knowing exactly where God was taking him. Joseph’s whole life changed when his brothers sold him into slavery. Ruth left her home and began again in a foreign land. David went from looking after sheep, to living in a palace, to running for his life. The disciples left their old lives to follow Jesus, often without knowing what the next day would bring.

What they discovered — and what we can discover too — is that when everything around us changes, God does not.

“Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever.”
— Hebrews 13:8

The Psalms often describe God as a rock. A rock is strong and steady. It does not move simply because the wind blows or the weather changes.

“The Lord is my rock, my fortress and my deliverer; my God is my rock, in whom I take refuge.”
— Psalm 18:2

That does not mean we will always immediately understand what is happening. Sometimes we would like God to show us exactly how everything is going to work out. We want a map of the whole journey: Who will my friends be? Will I enjoy this? Why is that person acting differently? What happens next?

But Scripture gives us another picture:

“Your word is a lamp for my feet, a light on my path.”
— Psalm 119:105

A lamp carried on a dark path does not normally show you everything several miles ahead. It shows you the ground in front of you. You can see enough to take the next step.

Sometimes that is how God leads us. He does not give us every answer at once. Instead, He asks us to trust Him today: to choose kindness today, to have courage today, to tell the truth today, to forgive today, to ask for help today. Then tomorrow, He gives us light for the next step.

Proverbs gives us a similar encouragement:

“Trust in the Lord with all your heart and lean not on your own understanding; in all your ways submit to him, and he will make your paths straight.”
— Proverbs 3:5–6

Notice that it does not say we will always understand the path. It says we can trust God as we walk it.

This is especially important when other people change. We cannot control what somebody else thinks, says or does. We cannot force another person to remain the same. But we can ask God to help us decide how we will respond. We can still be kind without allowing someone to treat us badly. We can still forgive while asking a parent, teacher or trusted adult for help. We can be patient when someone is having a difficult time, while remembering that their choices do not have to determine ours.

And when we feel unsettled, God invites us to bring that uncertainty to Him.

“God is our refuge and strength, an ever-present help in trouble. Therefore we will not fear…”
— Psalm 46:1–2

Things around us will change many times during our lives. Schools change. Friendships change. Families grow. Plans change. We grow and change too. Some changes will be exciting and others will be difficult.

But underneath all of those things is something that does not move.

God is still God. He is still good. He is still with us. His Word is still a light for our path, and He is still our rock.

So when we cannot see the whole road ahead, perhaps we do not need to.

We can ask a simpler question:

“God, what is the next right step you are asking me to take?”

And then, holding onto Him, we take it.

Talk about it
#

  • Is there anything changing at the moment that feels exciting, difficult or uncertain?
  • When something changes, what are you most likely to worry about?
  • Why do you think the Bible describes God as a rock?
  • What might it mean for God’s Word to be a lamp rather than a floodlight showing us everything ahead?
  • Is there one “next right step” you think God might be asking you to take this week?

Things to remember
#

Psalm 46 describes a world where even the earth and mountains seem to move, yet God remains a safe place in the middle of it all. Psalm 119 gives us another picture: God’s word is a lamp for our feet. We do not need to see the whole journey before we can trust Him with the next step.

Important message
#

When everything around us changes, God does not. We can trust Him for the next step even when we cannot see the whole road ahead.

Prayer
#

Father, thank you that you do not change even when the world around us does. When we are excited about something new, help us to enjoy it with you. When we feel unsure or frightened, remind us that you are our rock and our safe place.

Help us not to worry because we cannot see the whole road ahead. Give us enough light for the next step. Give us wisdom when friendships or circumstances change, courage when things feel unfamiliar, and kindness in the way we respond to other people.

Help us to trust you with all our hearts and to remember that wherever we go, you go with us.

Amen.

For younger ones
#

Read together Psalm 119:105 NIrV

Sometimes things change and we do not like it very much.

You might move to a new class. Someone might start playing with different friends. A place that used to feel easy might suddenly feel strange. When that happens, it can make your tummy feel funny or make you wonder, “What do I do now?”

The Bible tells us something very important: God does not change.

He is like a big, strong rock that does not move when everything around it feels wobbly.

The Bible also tells us that God’s word is like a lamp for our feet. Imagine walking outside when it is very dark and you have a little torch. You might not be able to see all the way to the end of the road, but you can see enough to take the next step.

That is sometimes how God helps us. We might not know everything that is going to happen tomorrow, but we can ask Him to help us with today.

Questions to ask

Can you think of one thing that feels different or new at the moment?

What is one small, brave step you could take?

Maybe the next step is to be kind. Maybe it is to say hello to someone new. Maybe it is to tell Mum, Dad or a teacher that something is worrying you.

Things to remember

Imagine holding a little torch in the dark. You do not need it to light up the whole road. You just need enough light to see where to put your foot next.

God can help us like that. We can trust Him one step at a time.

Important message

God is with me, so I do not have to work everything out on my own.

Let’s pray

Jesus, thank you that you are always with me.

When things change and I feel unsure, help me remember that you are strong and steady like a rock. Please show me the next step to take, and help me to be brave and kind.

Amen.
