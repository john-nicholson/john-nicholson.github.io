Be Strong and Courageous

13 September 2026·1039 words·5 mins

Read: Scripture Joshua 1:1–9 NIV

Tap or hover over the reference to read the full passage without leaving the page.

Joshua was facing something enormous. Moses had died, and after years of watching Moses lead Israel, Joshua was now responsible for leading the people into the land God had promised them. Thousands of people were looking to him, there were enemies ahead, and Joshua couldn’t possibly know exactly what was going to happen.

Into that uncertainty, God repeatedly tells him: “Be strong and courageous.”

What does being strong mean?
#

When you hear the word strong, what do you picture? Someone with enormous muscles? Someone who wins fights? Someone who isn’t frightened of anything? Someone who never cries?

What does being strong mean to you? Who do you know that you would describe as strong, and what makes them strong?

It’s easy to imagine that courage means not being scared. But if Joshua had nothing to fear, God wouldn’t need to keep telling him to be courageous. Joshua had plenty of reasons to be frightened. Courage mattered precisely because what he was being asked to do was difficult. Being courageous isn’t the same as having no fear. Sometimes courage means being afraid and choosing to do what is right anyway.

Where does Joshua’s courage come from?
#

God doesn’t simply tell Joshua to try harder to be brave. He gives him a reason to be courageous: “As I was with Moses, so I will be with you; I will never leave you nor forsake you.” Then, at the end of the passage, God says it again: “For the LORD your God will be with you wherever you go.”

Joshua’s confidence wasn’t supposed to come from believing that he was strong enough to handle everything. His courage came from knowing who was with him.

Right in the middle of these instructions, though, God also tells Joshua something else: “Keep this Book of the Law always on your lips; meditate on it day and night.” Joshua is told to keep returning to God’s word: to read it, talk about it, think about it and, importantly, do what it says. God’s word wasn’t supposed to be something Joshua occasionally consulted. It was meant to shape how he thought and how he lived.

There’s an important connection here. God tells Joshua to be courageous, reminds him of His presence, and tells him to keep His word continually before him. Joshua would face situations where what he could see gave him plenty of reasons to be afraid. God’s word reminded him of something greater than what he could see.

What about us?
#

Most of us aren’t preparing to lead a nation into the Promised Land, but we all face things that require courage. It might be something happening at school, a difficult friendship, trying something when you’re worried you’ll fail, standing up for someone, admitting you’ve done something wrong, or simply doing the right thing when everyone around you is doing something different.

So think of something happening in your life right now where you need courage. What would being “strong and courageous” actually look like in that situation? What are you frightened might happen? And what difference does it make to know that God is with you in it?

Perhaps that’s the most important thing to remember from Joshua’s story. Biblical courage isn’t pretending we’re not frightened, and strength isn’t convincing ourselves that we can handle everything alone.

We can be strong and courageous because God is with us.

And one of the ways we learn to trust Him, particularly when what we see around us gives us reasons to worry, is by continuing to come back to His word.

Prayer
#

Lord, thank you that you don’t ask us to face life on our own, and that you were with Joshua when he faced something far bigger than he could handle himself.

Help us to be strong and courageous, not because we think we can manage everything ourselves, but because we trust you. Give us a love for your word. Help us not simply to read it, but to think about it, talk about it and allow it to change the way we live.

Help us as a family to encourage one another, be honest with one another, and grow closer to you together.

Amen.

For younger ones
#

Read together Joshua 1:1–9 NIrV

Joshua had a really big job to do, and he might have been scared. But God told him: “Be strong and courageous.”

That didn’t mean Joshua had to be the strongest person or that he was never allowed to feel frightened. God told Joshua why he could be brave: God was going to be with him.

Questions to ask

Think about something that sometimes makes you feel scared. Maybe it’s going somewhere new, being in the dark, talking to someone you don’t know, trying something difficult, or doing the right thing when someone else doesn’t want you to.

Can you think of something that you have to be brave to do?

Now imagine doing that thing while holding the hand of someone you really trust. Does it feel a little different when you know you’re not doing it alone? That’s what God wanted Joshua to remember. Joshua had a big job to do, but he didn’t have to do it by himself. And neither do we.

Things to remember

Put your hand up and make a big muscle. That’s one kind of strong!

Now put your hand on your heart. There’s another kind of strength: choosing to do what is right even when you’re frightened.

Important message

Being brave doesn’t mean I’m never scared. I can be brave because God is with me.

Let’s pray

God, thank you that you are with me when I’m happy and when I’m scared. Please help me to be brave when something is difficult and help me to remember that I don’t have to face things on my own.

Help me to listen to you and learn more about you.

Amen.
