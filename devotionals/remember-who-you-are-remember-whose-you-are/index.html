Remember Who You Are — Remember Whose You Are

18 September 2026·748 words·4 mins
Fear

Read: Scripture Romans 4:16–21 NIV and Scripture Galatians 3:26–29 NIV
Read alongside: Scripture Psalm 105:1–11 NIV

There are moments when it helps to remember not only who we are, but whose we are.

Life changes. Our strength changes. Our responsibilities change. Sometimes even the way we see ourselves can change. But if you belong to Jesus, there is something deeper than all of that.

You belong to God.

You are part of his family. You are part of a story that began long before you were born and will continue long after your own lifetime.

Remember Abraham
#

Abraham learned to live by promise.

God had spoken to him years earlier and promised that his family would become something far greater than Abraham could yet see. But time passed. Abraham became older. Sarah became older. The promise looked less and less likely from a human point of view.

Romans says:

Scripture Romans 4:18 NIV

Abraham did not have confidence because everything around him looked easy. He had confidence because God had spoken.

And God had not forgotten his promise.

That matters because the New Testament tells us that the story of Abraham does not end with Abraham.

Paul writes:

Scripture Galatians 3:29 NIV

That means this story includes you.

You Belong to a Bigger Story
#

Your faith did not begin with you.

For generations, people have trusted God, prayed, worshipped, struggled, hoped, failed, started again and discovered that God remained faithful.

You are part of that same story.

That is part of your heritage in Christ.

There may be things about your own past that become harder to remember, but your place in God’s family does not depend on your ability to remember every detail.

You are still his.

You are still loved.

You are still an heir through Christ.

Read Psalm 105
#

Psalm 105 begins with an invitation:

Scripture Psalm 105:1 NIV

Then it tells God’s people to remember.

Scripture Psalm 105:5 NIV

The Psalm looks back over generations of God’s faithfulness. It reminds the people that they are not standing alone in the present moment. They are part of a covenant story.

That is important when the present feels uncertain.

Sometimes the best way to face today is to remember that God has been faithful before.

He was faithful to Abraham.

He was faithful to generations after him.

And he has been faithful to you.

Your Inheritance Is Secure
#

Galatians says:

Scripture Galatians 3:26 NIV

That is not something age can take away.

It is not something weakness can take away.

It is not something fear can take away.

And it is not something confusion can take away.

You belong to God because of what Christ has done, not because of how strong you feel today.

Your inheritance rests in him.

You Still Have a Place in the Story
#

Heritage is not only about looking backwards.

It is also about what we pass on.

You still have something to give.

You can still pray.

You can still speak about God’s faithfulness.

You can still bless your family.

You can still encourage someone younger.

You can still remind the next generation that God has been good.

The story is not finished with you.

Remember
#

I belong to Jesus.
I am part of God’s family.
I am part of a story of faith.
God has kept his promises before.
He has not forgotten me.
He is not finished with me.

Say this together
#

I belong to God.
I am his child.
I am part of his family.
God has been faithful.
God is faithful now.
God will remain faithful.
I do not need to be afraid.

Prayer
#

Father, thank you that I belong to you.

Thank you that my life is part of a much bigger story of faith and promise. Thank you for Abraham and for every generation that has trusted you before me.

When I feel uncertain, help me remember that your promises are not uncertain. When I forget things, remind me that you have not forgotten me.

Thank you that through Jesus I am your child and an heir of your promise.

Help me to keep trusting you and to pass on what I know of your faithfulness to those who come after me.

In Jesus’ name, Amen.
